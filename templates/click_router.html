      Per-stop weights

      Give each stop a delivery weight (or volume — any consistent unit works). "Max load/vehicle" above then caps the TOTAL weight a vehicle carries, not its stop count. Any stop left blank defaults to 1.

        My routes

        Every route you solve in this browser is saved here automatically (no account, no server — just this browser's storage). Click Load to bring back its pins and settings, star it to keep it around no matter how many newer solves come after it.

          Precedence rules

          Require one stop to be visited before another — e.g. a pickup before its matching drop-off. This is a real constraint baked directly into the solve, not a post-hoc filter (see the README). Single-vehicle mode only; rules automatically follow your stops as the route is (re-)solved, but are cleared if you add or remove a stop, since positions shift.

          before
            Time windows

            Require a stop to be reached within an earliest/latest arrival window, in minutes from your Start point. This is pruned into the solve itself, then checked against the real solved schedule — see the README. Single-vehicle mode only, and only for method = Quantum-inspired or Real QPU (classical 2-opt has no notion of a position constraint) and only while the route has {{ cluster_size }} interior stops or fewer (past that it's solved as multiple independent clusters, and a window tied to the full route's order can't be enforced within one cluster's own local positions). Rules automatically follow your stops as the route is (re-)solved, but are cleared if you add or remove a stop, since positions shift.

              Method comparison

              Solves your current stops with BOTH methods and shows the real difference on THIS instance — not a general claim either method always wins (see the README's benchmark experiments for aggregate comparisons across many random instances). Time-window rules, if any, aren't supported under Classical, so they're applied to the Quantum-inspired side only.

              Import stops

              Paste one stop per line — an address/place name, or a direct lat, lon pair (e.g. 18.5679, 73.7143). Addresses are geocoded against the selected city, the same way the search box above works. Or upload a .csv/.txt file with the same format, one stop per line.

              Insights & Benchmark Dashboard

              The project's real, honestly-measured finding: classical routing already wins on plain, unconstrained routing — the quantum-inspired formulation earns its place once a real dispatch constraint enters the picture. Every chart below is built live from the same CSVs in output/ that src/benchmark.py writes — nothing here is a mockup or a cherry-picked run. Full write-up: docs/benchmarks.md.
